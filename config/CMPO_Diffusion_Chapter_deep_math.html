Motivation: the multiplier that never settles

Every Lagrangian safe-RL method — SAC-Lag, PPO-Lag, FOCOPS, and the reachability-constrained policy gradient of Chapter 9 — shares one structural weakness: the dual variable is learned, by gradient ascent, at the same time as the policy. Neither ever sees the other at optimality.

Write the constrained MDP problem in its saddle-point form,

\[ \max_\theta \min_{\lambda \ge 0}\; J_r(\pi_\theta) \;-\; \lambda\,\big(J_c(\pi_\theta) - d\big), \]

and the standard recipe is a simultaneous stochastic ascent–descent: one noisy gradient step on \(\theta\), one noisy gradient step on \(\lambda\), repeat. The multiplier chases a moving policy; the policy chases a moving penalty. The resulting dynamics are those of a damped oscillator at best, and of a limit cycle at worst: cost overshoots the budget, \(\lambda\) winds up, the policy over-corrects into conservatism, \(\lambda\) collapses, and the cycle repeats. For state-wise constraints the situation is worse still — when \(\lambda\) becomes a multiplier network \(\lambda_\phi(s)\) trained by dual ascent, local overshoots propagate to neighbouring states through the function approximator and amplify the oscillation (ALaM documents exactly this failure mode).

There is a second, quieter weakness. In the Lagrangian-backprop approach of Chapter 9 the safety critic \(Q_c\) enters the policy update through the chain rule: \(\nabla_\theta\, \lambda\, Q_c(s, \pi_\theta(s))\), differentiated through the whole denoising chain of the diffusion policy. The gradient is only as good as \(\nabla_a Q_c\) — a quantity the critic was never trained to get right — and for a \(K\)-step diffusion policy it is propagated through \(K\) compositions before it touches \(\theta\).

What Lagrangian methods do

One inexact gradient step on \(\lambda\) per iteration. The multiplier is a state of the optimizer, with its own learning rate, its own momentum, its own failure modes. The constraint is enforced only in the limit — if the dynamics converge at all.

What this chapter does instead

Solve the constrained policy-improvement step exactly, in closed form, per iteration — over a nonparametric distribution where the problem is convex. The multiplier is not learned; it is computed, fresh, each iteration, by a two-dimensional convex program.

↑ the design choice this chapter develops — exact dual solves instead of dual dynamics

The idea is not exotic. It is the natural marriage of two observations. First, MPO (Abdolmaleki et al., 2018) already solves its policy-improvement step exactly over a nonparametric distribution: for each state it reweights sampled actions by \(\exp(Q/\eta)\), where the temperature \(\eta\) is the exact solution of a one-dimensional convex dual. Second, an expected-cost constraint is linear in that nonparametric distribution — so adding it to the E-step keeps the problem convex, keeps the closed form, and merely upgrades the dual from one dimension to two. The reweighting becomes

\[ q^*(a\mid s)\;\propto\;\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid s)\, \exp\!\Big(\tfrac{Q_r(s,a)-\lambda\,Q_c(s,a)}{\eta}\Big), \]

with \((\eta,\lambda)\) the exact minimizer of a jointly convex dual. This is CVPO (Liu et al., ICML 2022) — and §4 derives it line by line. The remainder of the chapter then does what CVPO did not: replaces the Gaussian policy class with a diffusion policy (§7), replaces the intractable action-space KL with the path-space trust region of BDPO/DIME/TruDi (§8), allows the multiplier to become state-dependent (§5), makes the budget relative to the current policy's cost so that the problem is always feasible (§6), and assembles the result into a concrete algorithm (§9).

The central idea

The constraint lives where the problem is convex

Policy improvement is split into two phases. In the E-step the policy is a free measure \(q(\cdot\mid s)\): expected reward is linear in \(q\), expected cost is linear in \(q\), the KL trust region is convex in \(q\) — so the safety-constrained improvement is a convex program with a closed-form solution and an exactly solvable two-dimensional dual. In the M-step the network only has to imitate \(q^*\) by weighted maximum likelihood — supervised learning, where deep networks are at their most reliable. The constraint never meets a policy gradient.

Provenance. The proposal analysed in this chapter is the E-step-constrained scheme suggested in discussion by G. Neumann (July 2026): apply the safety constraint inside MPO's weighted-maximum-likelihood machinery — "the constraint is linear in the sample weighting, so it can be solved rather than penalized" — with state-dependent multipliers and inference-time guidance as extensions. The chapter's contribution is to make every step of that sketch precise, to locate it in the literature (it recovers CVPO almost exactly), and to identify what remains genuinely open.

Background I — convex duality in three lemmas

Everything in this chapter reduces to three pieces of convex analysis: the solution of a linear objective under a KL constraint, the convexity of the resulting dual, and the behaviour of that dual when the primal is infeasible. We state them once, in full, and reuse them everywhere.

2.1 The Gibbs variational principle

Lemma 2.1 — Donsker–Varadhan / Gibbs

Let \(\mu\) be a probability measure on \(\mathcal A\) and \(f:\mathcal A\to\mathbb R\) measurable with \(\mathbb E_\mu[e^{f}]<\infty\). Then over probability measures \(q \ll \mu\),

\[ \sup_{q}\;\Big\{\,\mathbb E_q[f] \;-\; \mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,\mu)\,\Big\} \;=\; \log \mathbb E_{\mu}\big[e^{f}\big], \]

and the supremum is attained uniquely at the Gibbs measure \(q^*(a) \propto \mu(a)\,e^{f(a)}\).

Proof

Write \(Z=\mathbb E_\mu[e^f]\) and let \(g(a)=\mu(a)e^{f(a)}/Z\). For any \(q\ll\mu\),

\[ \mathbb E_q[f]-\mathrm{KL}(q\|\mu) = \mathbb E_q\!\Big[\log\tfrac{\mu e^{f}}{q}\Big] = \log Z - \mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,g)\;\le\;\log Z, \]

with equality iff \(q=g\), since \(\mathrm{KL}\ge 0\) with equality iff the arguments agree. ∎

Lemma 2.1 is the engine of every "exponentiated-advantage" formula in this chapter: whenever a linear functional of \(q\) is maximized against a KL penalty to a reference \(\mu\), the optimum tilts \(\mu\) exponentially by the integrand. The KL-constrained version follows by Lagrangian duality and inherits a temperature.

2.2 The temperature dual and its convexity

Lemma 2.2 — perspective of log-sum-exp

For fixed \(f\), the function

\[ h(\eta)\;=\;\eta\,\log \mathbb E_{\mu}\!\big[e^{f/\eta}\big],\qquad \eta>0, \]

is convex in \(\eta\); it is the perspective of the convex function \(t\mapsto\log\mathbb E_\mu[e^{tf}]\big|_{t=1}\) evaluated at \((\eta,1)\). More generally, for \(f_\lambda(a)=Q_r(a)-\lambda\,Q_c(a)\) affine in \(\lambda\), the map

\[ (\eta,\lambda)\;\longmapsto\;\eta\,\log \mathbb E_{\mu}\!\big[e^{(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta}\big] \]

is jointly convex on \(\{\eta>0,\ \lambda\in\mathbb R\}\).

Proof

Step 1. \(\Phi(g)=\log\mathbb E_\mu[e^{g}]\) is convex in the function \(g\) (Hölder's inequality: \(\Phi(\alpha g_1+(1-\alpha)g_2)\le\alpha\Phi(g_1)+(1-\alpha)\Phi(g_2)\)).

Step 2. \(g_\lambda = Q_r-\lambda Q_c\) is affine in \(\lambda\), so \(\lambda\mapsto\Phi(g_\lambda)\) is convex (convex ∘ affine).

Step 3. The perspective operation \((\eta,\lambda)\mapsto \eta\,\Phi(g_\lambda/\eta)\cdot 1 = \eta\,\log\mathbb E_\mu[e^{(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta}]\) preserves convexity: if \(\psi(\lambda)\) is convex then \((\eta,\lambda)\mapsto\eta\,\psi(\lambda/\eta)\) is jointly convex for \(\eta>0\) (Boyd & Vandenberghe, §3.2.6). Here the joint argument is \((\eta,\lambda)\) through the scaled exponent. ∎

The practical content: the dual problems of §3 and §4 are convex in all their dual variables jointly. They can be solved to machine precision each iteration by a handful of Newton or SLSQP steps on a Monte-Carlo estimate. There is no saddle-point dance.

2.3 Duality gap and the smell of infeasibility

Lemma 2.3 — unbounded dual ⇔ empty primal

Consider \(\max_q \mathbb E_q[Q_r]\) subject to \(\mathbb E_q[Q_c]\le d\), \(\mathrm{KL}(q\|\mu)\le\varepsilon\), over probability measures \(q\ll\mu\). If \(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{a\sim\mu} Q_c(a) > d\), the primal is infeasible, and the dual objective satisfies

\[ g(\eta,\lambda)\;\xrightarrow[\lambda\to\infty]{}\;-\infty \quad\text{along the recession direction } (\eta \text{ fixed},\,\lambda\uparrow), \]

i.e. the dual is unbounded below. Conversely, if some \(q\) in the KL ball satisfies the cost budget strictly (Slater's condition), strong duality holds and the dual attains a finite minimizer \((\eta^*,\lambda^*)\).

Proof sketch

With \(g(\eta,\lambda)=\lambda d+\eta\varepsilon+\eta\log\mathbb E_\mu[e^{(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta}]\) (derived in §4), the log-partition term behaves for large \(\lambda\) as \(-\lambda\operatorname*{ess\,inf}Q_c + O(1)\) (Laplace principle: the expectation concentrates on the minimizers of \(Q_c\)). Hence \(g \sim \lambda\,(d-\operatorname*{ess\,inf}Q_c)\to-\infty\) precisely when \(d<\operatorname*{ess\,inf}Q_c\). Slater ⇒ strong duality is standard for convex programs over probability measures with a KL ball of positive radius. ∎

Lemma 2.3 is not a pathology to be feared — it is a feature. A diverging \(\lambda\) in the per-state dual is a certificate, available at essentially zero cost, that the state is infeasible at the current budget: no reweighting of the current policy's actions can satisfy the constraint there. Section 6 turns this certificate into a switching rule.

Background II — policy iteration as EM: MPO

MPO recasts policy improvement as inference: posit a "success" event whose likelihood grows with \(Q\), infer the posterior over actions given success, then fit the network to that posterior. The E-step is the inference; the M-step is weighted maximum likelihood. Everything downstream of this chapter is a modification of one of these two steps.

3.1 Control as inference, minimally

Introduce a binary optimality variable \(O\) with \(p(O=1\mid s,a)\propto\exp\!\big(Q^{\pi_{\text{old}}}(s,a)/\eta\big)\). Conditioning the old policy on \(O=1\) and applying Bayes gives the posterior

\[ p(a\mid s, O=1)\;\propto\;\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid s)\, \exp\!\big(Q^{\pi_{\text{old}}}(s,a)/\eta\big) \]

— exactly the Gibbs tilt of Lemma 2.1 with \(\mu=\pi_{\text{old}}\), \(f=Q/\eta\). MPO's insight is to treat the temperature \(\eta\) not as a hyperparameter but as the Lagrange multiplier of an explicit trust-region constraint, which the following program makes precise.

3.2 The E-step as a constrained program

Definition 3.1 — nonparametric E-step (MPO)

Given a state distribution \(\rho\) (in practice: states sampled from replay), the old policy \(\pi_{\text{old}}\), and a critic \(Q_r \approx Q^{\pi_{\text{old}}}_r\), solve over per-state probability densities \(q(\cdot\mid s)\):

\[ \max_{q}\;\; \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\,\mathbb E_{a\sim q(\cdot\mid s)}\big[Q_r(s,a)\big] \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\big[\mathrm{KL}\big(q(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot\mid s)\big)\big]\le\varepsilon, \qquad \textstyle\int q(a\mid s)\,da = 1 \;\;\forall s. \]

The objective is linear in \(q\); the KL constraint is convex in \(q\); the normalization is affine. This is a convex program over an infinite-dimensional simplex — and it has a closed-form solution.

Theorem 3.2 — MPO E-step, closed form and dual

The unique solution of Definition 3.1 is

\[ q^*(a\mid s)\;=\;\frac{\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid s)\,\exp\!\big(Q_r(s,a)/\eta^*\big)}{Z_{\eta^*}(s)}, \qquad Z_\eta(s)=\int \pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid s)\,e^{Q_r(s,a)/\eta}\,da, \]

where \(\eta^*>0\) minimizes the convex dual

\[ g(\eta)\;=\;\eta\,\varepsilon\;+\;\eta\;\mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\Big[\log Z_\eta(s)\Big]. \]
Proof

Form the Lagrangian with multipliers \(\eta\ge 0\) (KL) and \(\kappa(s)\) (normalization):

\[ L(q,\eta,\kappa)=\mathbb E_\rho\!\int\! q\,Q_r +\eta\Big(\varepsilon-\mathbb E_\rho\!\int\! q\log\tfrac{q}{\pi_{\text{old}}}\Big) +\int\!\kappa(s)\Big(1-\!\int\! q\Big). \]

Pointwise stationarity in \(q(a\mid s)\): \(Q_r-\eta\log\frac{q}{\pi_{\text{old}}}-\eta-\kappa(s)=0\), i.e. \(q\propto\pi_{\text{old}}\,e^{Q_r/\eta}\); the normalizer absorbs \(\kappa\). Substituting back and using \(\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_r]-\eta\,\mathrm{KL}(q^*\|\pi_{\text{old}})=\eta\log Z_\eta(s)\) (Lemma 2.1 with \(f=Q_r/\eta\), scaled) yields \(g(\eta)=\eta\varepsilon+\eta\,\mathbb E_\rho[\log Z_\eta]\). Convexity of \(g\) is Lemma 2.2 with \(\lambda\) frozen. Strong duality holds because the KL ball has nonempty interior (\(q=\pi_{\text{old}}\) is Slater). ∎

Two remarks that matter later. First, the dual is a one-dimensional convex problem, estimated by sampling \(L\) states from replay and \(M\) actions per state from \(\pi_{\text{old}}\), replacing \(Z_\eta(s)\) by \(\frac1M\sum_j e^{Q_r(s,a_j)/\eta}\) — a log-sum-exp over the action samples, numerically stabilized by subtracting the max. Second, the gradient of the dual has KKT meaning:

\[ g'(\eta)\;=\;\varepsilon\;-\;\mathbb E_\rho\big[\mathrm{KL}(q^*_\eta\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}})\big], \]

so the exact minimizer \(\eta^*\) is precisely the temperature at which the reweighted distribution uses up its KL budget. The multiplier is computed to make the constraint tight — the geometric picture worth holding on to before we add safety.

3.3 The M-step: weighted maximum likelihood

The E-step's \(q^*\) is nonparametric — it exists only as weights on sampled actions. The M-step projects it back onto the policy class by minimizing the forward KL,

\[ \theta_{\text{new}} =\arg\min_\theta\;\mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\Big[\mathrm{KL}\big(q^*(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)\big)\Big] =\arg\max_\theta\;\mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\,\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_{\text{old}}}\Big[\underbrace{w(s,a)}_{q^*/\pi_{\text{old}}}\,\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\Big], \]

i.e. weighted maximum likelihood with importance weights \(w(s,a)=e^{Q_r(s,a)/\eta^*}/Z(s)\) on actions already sampled from the old policy. MPO additionally bounds \(\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\text{old}}\|\pi_\theta)\le\beta\) in the M-step to keep the parametric projection itself inside a trust region. For a Gaussian policy, \(\log\pi_\theta\) is exact and this is textbook supervised learning. For a diffusion policy it is not — that is the business of §7.

The EM analogy is exact: the E-step computes a variational posterior (here: the optimal reweighting under the trust region), the M-step maximizes the expected log-likelihood under it. As in EM, monotonic improvement holds when both steps are solved exactly — and degrades gracefully to "approximate improvement" when the M-step is a bounded surrogate. Keep this in mind: it is where the diffusion policy will tax us.

3.4 Cousins: MO-MPO, V-MPO, AWR

Three variants matter for orientation. MO-MPO (Abdolmaleki et al., ICML 2020) runs one E-step per objective, each with its own KL budget \(\varepsilon_k\) — preferences are encoded as "how much influence may objective \(k\) exert", not as hard budgets \(d_k\); safety-as-preference is expressible but never guaranteed. V-MPO (Song et al., 2019) is the on-policy variant using a state-value function and top-half advantages. AWR/AWAC drop the exact dual and fix \(\eta\) as a hyperparameter — cheaper, but they surrender exactly the property this chapter is built on: that multipliers are computed, not tuned. The hard-constraint member of the family is CVPO, next.

4

The constrained E-step — full derivation

Add one line to Definition 3.1 — an expected-cost budget — and re-derive. Because the new constraint is linear in \(q\), the program stays convex, the Gibbs form survives with a tilted exponent, and the dual grows from one dimension to two. This section is the mathematical heart of the chapter.

Definition 4.1 — constrained E-step (CVPO)

Given reward and cost critics \(Q_r, Q_c\) for \(\pi_{\text{old}}\), a cost budget \(d\), and KL radius \(\varepsilon\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{q}\;\; & \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\,\mathbb E_{a\sim q}\big[Q_r(s,a)\big]\\[2pt] \text{s.t.}\;\; & \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\,\mathbb E_{a\sim q}\big[Q_c(s,a)\big]\;\le\; d,\\ & \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\big[\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}})\big]\;\le\;\varepsilon, \qquad \textstyle\int q(a\mid s)\,da=1\;\;\forall s. \end{aligned} \]
Theorem 4.2 — closed form and two-dimensional dual

Assume Slater's condition (some \(q\) in the KL ball satisfies the budget strictly). The unique solution of Definition 4.1 is

\[ q^*(a\mid s)\;=\;\frac{\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid s)\, \exp\!\Big(\dfrac{Q_r(s,a)-\lambda^* Q_c(s,a)}{\eta^*}\Big)}{Z_{\eta^*,\lambda^*}(s)}, \qquad Z_{\eta,\lambda}(s)=\int\!\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid s)\,e^{(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta}\,da, \]

where \((\eta^*,\lambda^*)\in\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb R_{\ge 0}\) is the unique minimizer of the jointly convex dual

\[ g(\eta,\lambda)\;=\;\lambda\,d\;+\;\eta\,\varepsilon\;+\;\eta\; \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\Big[\log Z_{\eta,\lambda}(s)\Big]. \]

Moreover the partial derivatives recover the KKT residuals:

\[ \frac{\partial g}{\partial\lambda} = d-\mathbb E_\rho\,\mathbb E_{q^*_{\eta,\lambda}}\!\big[Q_c\big], \qquad \frac{\partial g}{\partial\eta} = \varepsilon-\mathbb E_\rho\big[\mathrm{KL}(q^*_{\eta,\lambda}\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}})\big]. \]
Proof

Stationarity. Lagrangian with \(\lambda\ge0\) (cost), \(\eta\ge0\) (KL), \(\kappa(s)\) (normalization):

\[ L=\mathbb E_\rho\!\int\! q\,Q_r -\lambda\Big(\mathbb E_\rho\!\int\! q\,Q_c - d\Big) +\eta\Big(\varepsilon-\mathbb E_\rho\!\int\! q\log\tfrac{q}{\pi_{\text{old}}}\Big) +\int\!\kappa(s)\Big(1-\!\int\! q\Big). \]

\(\partial L/\partial q(a\mid s)=0\) gives \(Q_r-\lambda Q_c-\eta\log\frac{q}{\pi_{\text{old}}}-\eta-\kappa(s)=0\), hence the Gibbs form with exponent \((Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta\).

Dual. Substituting \(q^*_{\eta,\lambda}\) back into \(L\) and applying Lemma 2.1 with \(f=(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta\):

\[ \mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_r-\lambda Q_c]-\eta\,\mathrm{KL}(q^*\|\pi_{\text{old}}) =\eta\log Z_{\eta,\lambda}(s) \;\Longrightarrow\; g(\eta,\lambda)=\lambda d+\eta\varepsilon+\eta\,\mathbb E_\rho[\log Z_{\eta,\lambda}]. \]

Convexity. Joint convexity in \((\eta,\lambda)\) is Lemma 2.2 (perspective of log-sum-exp of an exponent affine in \(\lambda\)); the linear terms \(\lambda d+\eta\varepsilon\) preserve it. Under Slater, strong duality and dual attainment are standard; uniqueness follows from strict convexity of the log-partition term whenever \(Q_c\) is not \(\pi_{\text{old}}\)-a.s. constant per state (non-degeneracy).

Derivatives. Differentiate \(\eta\log Z_{\eta,\lambda}\) under the integral: \(\partial_\lambda\big[\eta\log Z\big]=-\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_c]\), and \(\partial_\eta\big[\eta\log Z\big]=\log Z-\tfrac1\eta\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_r-\lambda Q_c]=-\mathrm{KL}(q^*\|\pi_{\text{old}})\) after one line of algebra using the Gibbs identity. ∎

Read the KKT derivatives as an interpretable thermostat. At the optimum, either \(\lambda^*=0\) and the reweighted policy already respects the budget (constraint inactive), or \(\lambda^*>0\) and \(\mathbb E_\rho\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_c]=d\) exactly — complementary slackness holds not asymptotically, not in expectation over training, but at every single iteration. This is the precise sense in which the E-step approach eliminates multiplier dynamics: the inner problem that Lagrangian methods solve by a slow outer loop of dual ascent is here solved to optimality, per iteration, in a 2-D convex program that costs a few Newton steps.

4.1 Monte-Carlo estimation of the dual

Sample \(L\) states \(s_i\sim\mathcal D\) and \(M\) actions \(a_{ij}\sim\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot\mid s_i)\); evaluate both critics once per pair. The empirical dual is

\[ \hat g(\eta,\lambda) =\lambda d+\eta\varepsilon +\frac{\eta}{L}\sum_{i=1}^L \log\!\Big(\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M \exp\!\Big(\tfrac{Q_r(s_i,a_{ij})-\lambda Q_c(s_i,a_{ij})}{\eta}\Big)\Big), \]

minimized over \(\{\eta\ge\eta_{\min},\ \lambda\in[0,\lambda_{\max}]\}\) by SLSQP or projected Newton — CVPO uses an off-the-shelf convex solver here. Numerics: always subtract \(\max_j(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta\) inside the log-sum-exp; cap \(\lambda_{\max}\) to detect the divergence of Lemma 2.3 rather than chase it. The resulting per-sample weights,

\[ w_{ij}\;=\;\frac{\exp\big((Q_r(s_i,a_{ij})-\lambda^* Q_c(s_i,a_{ij}))/\eta^*\big)} {\sum_{j'}\exp\big((Q_r(s_i,a_{ij'})-\lambda^* Q_c(s_i,a_{ij'}))/\eta^*\big)}, \]

are all the M-step ever sees. Note what did not happen: no gradient of \(Q_c\) with respect to actions, no backprop through a sampler, no multiplier network. The critics are consulted only through their values at sampled actions — which is also why the scheme is natively off-policy: replay actions are fine as long as importance-consistent \(\pi_{\text{old}}\)-samples (or a correction) are used.

Honest accounting — where CVPO pays

The exactness is bought with compute. The E-step requires \(M\) fresh action samples and \(2LM\) critic evaluations per update, plus a convex solve; the SDAC paper's timing table reports CVPO as by far the slowest safe-RL baseline in wall-clock (≈47 h vs ≈2.6 h for CPO on Safety-Gym PointGoal at 5M steps, their Table 5). For a Gaussian policy the sampling is trivial and the cost is the solver; for a \(K\)-step diffusion policy each of the \(M\) samples costs \(K\) network passes. §7.4 and §9 treat this as a first-order design constraint, not a footnote.

Verdict: exact duals are cheap in dimensions, expensive in samples. Budget for it.

5

State-dependent multipliers λ(s)

A single scalar \(\lambda\) enforces the budget on average over states — a dangerous averaging when safety is local. The E-step decouples across states, so the constraint can be made state-wise and each state can solve its own tiny dual. The question is not whether this is possible (it is, trivially) but whether it is statistically affordable.

5.1 The per-state program

Replace the aggregated constraints by per-state ones — budget \(d(s)\) and KL radius \(\varepsilon(s)\):

\[ \text{for each } s:\qquad \max_{q(\cdot\mid s)}\;\mathbb E_q[Q_r] \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb E_q[Q_c]\le d(s),\;\; \mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}})\le\varepsilon(s). \]

Because nothing couples the states, Theorem 4.2 applies verbatim per state, giving state-dependent optimal duals \(\big(\eta^*(s),\lambda^*(s)\big)\) and per-state weights with exponent \(\big(Q_r-\lambda^*(s)\,Q_c\big)/\eta^*(s)\). CVPO itself derives this "batch-size-one" dual \(\bar g\) in its appendix — and then deliberately avoids it, on the grounds that per-state duals are computationally expensive and sensitive to critic error. Both objections are quantifiable.

5.2 The statistics of a per-state dual

Each per-state dual is estimated from only \(M\) action samples. The gradient component that determines \(\lambda^*(s)\) is \(d(s)-\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_c]\), a self-normalized importance-weighted mean over \(M\) samples. Its variance scales as

\[ \operatorname{Var}\big[\widehat{\mathbb E}_{q^*}[Q_c]\big] \;\approx\;\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{q^*}[Q_c]}{M_{\text{eff}}}, \qquad M_{\text{eff}}=\frac{\big(\sum_j w_j\big)^2}{\sum_j w_j^2}\;\le\;M, \]

and \(M_{\text{eff}}\) collapses exactly when the tilt is aggressive — low temperature \(\eta\), large \(\lambda\), i.e. near-critical states, which is where you most need the multiplier to be right. Add critic error: a bias \(b(s,a)\) in \(Q_c\) shifts the constraint surface itself, and the per-state \(\lambda^*(s)\) — unlike the batch \(\lambda^*\), which averages \(b\) over \(L\) states — inherits it at full strength. The batch dual trades locality for an \(O(\sqrt{L})\) variance reduction. That is the entire trade.

5.3 Three implementable compromises

A · Shared η, per-state λ(s)

Temperature is a global exploration knob; safety is local. Solve one 1-D dual for \(\eta\) at fixed \(\lambda(\cdot)\), then \(L\) independent 1-D duals for \(\lambda(s_i)\). Each 1-D solve is monotone root-finding on \(\partial g/\partial\lambda\) — bisection suffices.

B · Amortized λφ(s) by regression

Fit a network to the per-state KKT targets \(\lambda^*(s_i)\) computed by convex solve. Crucially this is regression to an exactly computed target, not dual ascent — the pathology ALaM documents (multiplier-network oscillation) comes from the ascent dynamics, not from parameterizing λ.

C · Bucketed λ by safety level

Partition states by a cheap statistic (e.g. \(\min_j Q_c(s,a_{ij})\) or the HJ value \(V_h(s)\) of Ch. 9) into \(B\) buckets; one λ per bucket. Interpolates the variance/locality trade with \(B\) as the dial. \(B=1\) is CVPO; \(B=L\) is fully state-wise.

↑ default to A; escalate to B only if per-state specs demand smooth λ(s); use C when the HJ value function already exists

The conceptual link to Chapter 9: state-dependent multipliers are the E-step analogue of the state-wise Lagrangian in Feasible Actor-Critic and Reachability-Constrained RL, where \(\lambda(s)\) is a function to be learned. Here it is a function to be evaluated — the per-state KKT system is solved fresh each iteration, so there is no multiplier lag and no propagation of overshoot through a function approximator, at the price of estimation variance that must be respected.

6

Infeasibility, recession, and improvement budgets

Two failure surfaces remain in the E-step itself: states where no reweighting satisfies the budget, and the global choice of budget when the initial policy is far from safe. Both have clean fixes, and one of them — the relative budget — was the professor's own suggestion.

6.1 Infeasible states: detect, then switch objectives

By Lemma 2.3, if \(\min_j Q_c(s_i,a_{ij}) > d(s_i)\) the per-state primal is empty and the per-state dual diverges: \(\lambda(s_i)\uparrow\lambda_{\max}\) with residual \(d - \mathbb E_{q}[Q_c]\) still negative. The cap \(\lambda_{\max}\) is therefore a detector. Upon detection, do not fight the geometry — change the objective for that state:

\[ \text{infeasible } s:\qquad \max_{q}\;\mathbb E_q\big[-Q_c(s,a)\big] \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathrm{KL}(q\|\pi_{\text{old}})\le\varepsilon \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; q^*\propto \pi_{\text{old}}\,e^{-Q_c/\eta}, \]

i.e. pure cost descent inside the trust region — recover toward feasibility as fast as the KL ball allows, reward be damned. This is precisely the rectification logic of CRPO (alternate reward improvement with constraint correction, no dual at all) and the feasibility split of FISOR (optimize reward in the feasible region; minimize the safety value in the infeasible region, where FISOR's regions come from HJ reachability). The E-step version costs one branch in the weight computation. The alternative — relaxing \(d(s)\) up to \(\min_j \hat Q_c + \text{slack}\) — is gentler but hides the infeasibility rather than repairing it; prefer the switch.

6.2 Relative budgets: "the cost is 5.1 — just require 4.8"

An absolute budget \(d_{\text{target}}\) imposed from iteration 0 is typically globally infeasible early in training: the initial policy's cost \(J_c(\pi_0)\) may sit far above it, and the E-step would immediately enter the recession regime almost everywhere. The professor's suggestion is the natural repair — constrain improvement, not level:

Proposition 6.1 — monotone-improvement budget schedule

Set, at iteration \(k\),

\[ d_k\;=\;\max\Big(d_{\text{target}},\;\hat J_c(\pi_k)-\delta_k\Big), \qquad \delta_k>0. \]

Then (i) the E-step at iteration \(k\) is feasible whenever \(\delta_k\) is small relative to the cost-decrease achievable inside the KL ball — in particular \(q=\pi_{\text{old}}\) violates \(d_k\) by at most \(\delta_k\), so a Slater point exists for \(\delta_k\) below the trust-region cost margin; (ii) if each E/M cycle realizes its constraint (up to M-step projection error \(\zeta_k\)), the cost sequence satisfies \(J_c(\pi_{k+1})\le J_c(\pi_k)-\delta_k+\zeta_k\), a monotone descent toward \(d_{\text{target}}\) at rate \(\sum_k(\delta_k-\zeta_k)\).

Proof sketch

(i) Feasibility: the reference \(q=\pi_{\text{old}}\) has \(\mathbb E_\rho\mathbb E_{\pi_{\text{old}}}[Q_c]=\hat J_c(\pi_k)\) (advantage identity at zero KL), which exceeds \(d_k\) by at most \(\delta_k\); continuity of \(\mathbb E_q[Q_c]\) in \(q\) inside the KL ball plus a nonzero descent direction (exists unless \(\pi_{\text{old}}\) is already cost-minimal per state) yields a strictly feasible point for small \(\delta_k\). (ii) Chain the E-step's exact constraint \(\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_c]\le d_k\) with the M-step's projection guarantee \(|J_c(\pi_{\theta_{k+1}})-\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_c]|\le\zeta_k\) (bounded by the M-step KL radius β via the usual performance-difference/TV argument, cf. Ch. 3 §4). ∎

The schedule has the flavour of CPO's guarantee ("each update is at least as safe as the last, minus a slack") but obtained by construction of the budget rather than by a trust-region surrogate bound — and it composes with the absolute target: once \(\hat J_c\) crosses \(d_{\text{target}}\), the max switches over and the constraint becomes absolute. Practical rule for \(\delta_k\): couple it to the observed feasible fraction — shrink \(\delta\) when the infeasibility detector of §6.1 fires on more than a few percent of states, grow it when \(\lambda^*\approx 0\) (constraint slack, free progress available).

One caution: \(\hat J_c(\pi_k)\) is itself an estimate; setting the budget relative to a noisy level injects that noise into the constraint. Use a slow-moving (EMA) estimate of \(J_c\), and never let \(d_k\) increase between iterations — clip to the running minimum.
7

Background III — diffusion policies and the weighted M-step

Everything so far was policy-class-agnostic: the E-step sees only samples and critic values. The policy class enters exactly once — in the M-step, which must maximize a weighted log-likelihood. For a diffusion policy the log-likelihood is intractable, and the honest question is what replacing it by the ELBO does to the projection.

7.1 Diffusion policies in five equations

A (DDPM-style) diffusion policy generates an action by reversing a fixed noising chain. Forward process on actions, conditioned on nothing:

\[ q(a^t\mid a^{t-1})=\mathcal N\big(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,a^{t-1},\,\beta_t I\big), \qquad q(a^t\mid a^0)=\mathcal N\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,a^0,\,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I\big), \]

with \(\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{u\le t}(1-\beta_u)\). The policy is the learned reverse chain, conditioned on the state:

\[ \pi_\theta(a^{0:T}\mid s)=p(a^T)\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_\theta(a^{t-1}\mid a^t,s), \qquad p_\theta(a^{t-1}\mid a^t,s)=\mathcal N\big(\mu_\theta(a^t,s,t),\,\sigma_t^2 I\big), \]

and the action marginal \(\pi_\theta(a^0\mid s)=\int\pi_\theta(a^{0:T}\mid s)\,da^{1:T}\) is the object the E-step reasons about — intractable, but bounded below by the ELBO:

\[ \log\pi_\theta(a^0\mid s)\;\ge\; \mathcal L_{\text{ELBO}}(a^0,s;\theta) \;=\;-\sum_{t}\mathbb E_{q}\Big[\mathrm{KL}\big(q(a^{t-1}\mid a^t,a^0)\,\|\,p_\theta(a^{t-1}\mid a^t,s)\big)\Big]+\text{const}, \]

which, in the standard \(\epsilon\)-parameterization, collapses to denoising score matching:

\[ -\mathcal L_{\text{ELBO}}(a^0,s;\theta)\;\overset{c}{=}\; \mathbb E_{t\sim\mathcal U,\;\epsilon\sim\mathcal N} \Big[\gamma_t\,\big\|\epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,a^0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\;s,\;t\big)-\epsilon\big\|^2\Big]. \]

7.2 The weighted denoising M-step

Definition 7.1 — weighted-ELBO projection

Given E-step weights \(w_{ij}\) on actions \(a_{ij}\sim\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot\mid s_i)\), the diffusion M-step minimizes

\[ \mathcal L_M(\theta)\;=\; \frac1L\sum_{i}\sum_{j} w_{ij}\; \mathbb E_{t,\epsilon}\Big[\gamma_t\big\|\epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,a_{ij}+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\;s_i,\;t\big)-\epsilon\big\|^2\Big]. \]

This is exactly the mechanism that online diffusion-RL methods already use for the unconstrained tilt: DPMD/SDAC realize policy mirror descent by reweighted score matching toward \(q^{\text{MD}}\propto\pi_{\text{old}}\,e^{Q/\eta}\); QVPO's Q-weighted variational objective is the same weighted VLO with weights from the reward critic alone. The constrained E-step changes only the weights — the exponent acquires \(-\lambda Q_c\). Machinery-wise, C-TruDi's M-step is a one-line diff on DPMD.

7.3 What the ELBO does to the projection — the bias, precisely

Proposition 7.2 — weighted-ELBO bias

Let \(G(\theta)=\mathbb E_{s}\mathbb E_{a\sim q^*}[\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)]\) be the true M-step objective and \(\tilde G(\theta)=\mathbb E_s\mathbb E_{a\sim q^*}[\mathcal L_{\text{ELBO}}(a,s;\theta)]\) the weighted-ELBO surrogate. Then

\[ G(\theta)-\tilde G(\theta) \;=\;\mathbb E_{s}\,\mathbb E_{a^0\sim q^*}\Big[ \mathrm{KL}\big(q(a^{1:T}\mid a^0)\,\big\|\,p_\theta(a^{1:T}\mid a^0,s)\big)\Big]\;\ge\;0, \]

i.e. the surrogate under-estimates the log-likelihood by the posterior mismatch of the diffusion chain, averaged under the E-step weights. Maximizing \(\tilde G\) therefore solves a projection with an additive, \(\theta\)-dependent, state-and-action-dependent slack — and the slack is largest exactly where the reverse chain fits worst, which the weights \(w\propto e^{(Q_r-\lambda Q_c)/\eta}\) may systematically up-weight (high-reward, low-cost tails).

Proof

Standard ELBO identity per sample: \(\log\pi_\theta(a^0\mid s)=\mathcal L_{\text{ELBO}}+\mathrm{KL}(q(a^{1:T}\mid a^0)\,\|\,p_\theta(a^{1:T}\mid a^0,s))\). Take \(\mathbb E_{q^*}\) of both sides. ∎

Consequences, stated without cosmetics. (i) Exact MPO's monotone-improvement argument needs the M-step to be a genuine I-projection; with the ELBO it becomes an approximate projection with gap \(\zeta\) equal to the weighted posterior mismatch, so the guarantee degrades to improvement-up-to-\(\zeta\) — the same currency in which DIME's convergence statement is denominated (a lower bound on the MaxEnt objective, not the objective itself). (ii) The cost side inherits the same slack: the realized \(J_c(\pi_{\theta_{\text{new}}})\) can exceed \(\mathbb E_{q^*}[Q_c]=d\) by an amount controlled by \(\zeta\) through the performance-difference lemma — this is the \(\zeta_k\) of Proposition 6.1, and the reason §9 keeps a deployment-time guard. (iii) QVPO's tightness analysis (conditions under which the Q-weighted VLO is a tight bound) is the right tool to import if the slack proves material; monitoring realized-vs-targeted cost is the cheap empirical proxy.

7.4 The sampling bill, and how to shrink it

The E-step wants \(M\) actions per state from the current policy; a \(K\)-step diffusion policy makes each one \(K\) network passes, so the per-iteration sampling bill is \(L\times M\times K\) forward passes before any training happens. Three mitigations, in increasing order of commitment: (a) few-step deterministic samplers (DDIM) for E-step sampling only, keeping the full chain for the M-step loss; (b) consistency distillation — CPQL reports ≈15×/45× train/inference speedups over Diffusion-QL at equal or better performance with a one-step consistency policy; (c) one-step flow policies — FPMD matches diffusion baselines with ~20× fewer inference steps. The structural point that makes all three admissible: the E-step needs samples and critic values, never gradients through the sampler — so a cheap distilled sampler for the E-step is exact-in-distribution up to distillation error, while the M-step continues to train the full-fidelity chain.

Trust regions in path space

MPO's M-step bounds \(\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\text{old}}\,\|\,\pi_\theta)\) between action marginals. For diffusion policies that KL is between two intractable integrals — but the KL between the generation processes is a sum of Gaussian terms. This is the trust-region object of BDPO, DIME and TruDi, and it slots into the M-step unchanged.

8.1 Marginal KL vs path KL

Lemma 8.1 — data-processing bound & decomposition

Let \(p_\theta(a^{0:T}\mid s)\) and \(p_{\text{old}}(a^{0:T}\mid s)\) be two reverse chains with the same prior \(p(a^T)\). Then

\[ \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_\theta(a^0\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}}(a^0\mid s)\big) \;\le\; \mathrm{KL}_{\text{path}} \;=\;\sum_{t=1}^{T}\;\mathbb E_{p_\theta}\Big[\mathrm{KL}\big(p_\theta(a^{t-1}\mid a^t,s)\,\|\,p_{\text{old}}(a^{t-1}\mid a^t,s)\big)\Big], \]

and each summand is a closed-form Gaussian KL, \(\tfrac{1}{2\sigma_t^2}\|\mu_\theta-\mu_{\text{old}}\|^2\) for shared variances. Bounding \(\mathrm{KL}_{\text{path}}\le\beta\) therefore bounds the marginal KL by \(\beta\).

Proof

Marginalization is a Markov map applied to the path measure; KL is non-increasing under Markov maps (data-processing inequality). The chain-rule decomposition of the path KL into per-step conditional KLs is exact for Markov chains sharing the prior. ∎

This is the tractability trade of the whole diffusion-trust-region literature: the path KL is an upper bound, generally strict (two different chains can induce the same marginal), so the trust region in path space is conservative in marginal space. BDPO proves the corresponding equivalence for its behavior-regularized objective and evaluates the accumulated discrepancy across denoising steps; TruDi enforces exactly such a trajectory-KL upper bound over the entire diffusion trajectory for on-policy trust-region updates; DIME's MaxEnt lower bound is denominated in the same path-space currency. For C-TruDi the M-step constraint is

\[ \min_\theta\;\mathcal L_M(\theta) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb E_{s\sim\rho}\big[\mathrm{KL}_{\text{path}}(\pi_\theta\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}})(s)\big]\;\le\;\beta, \]

enforced exactly as in TruDi (trajectory-KL bound with a Lagrangian on β) — reuse of in-house machinery, no new derivation needed.

8.2 Inference-time guidance: the constraint re-enters at sampling

The E-step constraint is in expectation under \(q^*\); individual sampled actions can still land in the costly tail, and the M-step slack \(\zeta\) (Prop. 7.2) widens that tail. Two mechanisms re-impose the constraint at generation time, both compatible with a trained C-TruDi policy:

Soft guidance. Shift each denoising mean by the cost gradient, \(\mu_\theta \mapsto \mu_\theta - \sigma_t^2\,\lambda_{\text{guide}}\nabla_a Q_c\) — classifier guidance with the cost critic as classifier. FISOR's refinement is relevant here: it deliberately uses time-independent guidance (built on the clean-action critic) because classifiers evaluated on noisy intermediate actions are unreliable; the Decoupled-Guidance line documents that cost signals on noisy latents correlate poorly and unstably with true cumulative cost. Guidance strength is a deployment knob, not a training-time correctness claim.

Hard shielding. Project the final sample (or every denoising iterate) onto the feasible set certified by a safety value function: HJ-reachability projection \(a\mapsto\arg\min_{a'\in\{V_h(s,a')\le 0\}}\|a-a'\|\), or SafeDiffuser's CBF-invariance embedded into the denoising dynamics. This is where Chapter 9's reachability apparatus composes cleanly with this chapter: the HJ layer needs only the executed action and \(V_h\) — it is agnostic to how the policy was trained, and it converts the E-step's soft, expected-cost safety into a hard, per-step, persistent guarantee. The known caveat carries over: HJ values certify remaining in the feasible set, not reaching it from outside — keep the cumulative-cost signal (and §6.1's recovery objective) for the infeasible region.

Division of labour

Three layers, three failure modes covered

E-step (training): optimal, non-myopic, expected-cost safety — exact per iteration. M-step slack: monitored, bounded by β and Prop. 7.2. Inference guard (deployment): hard, myopic, per-step safety via HJ/CBF — catches the tail the expectation misses. None of the three substitutes for the others; the composition is the method.

Critical audit — the three risks

The method is sound; it is not free. Three risks are structural rather than incidental, and each has a measurement that decides it.

Risk 1 — the projection is not a projection (weighted-ELBO bias)

Prop. 7.2: the M-step optimizes a lower bound whose gap is the posterior mismatch of the diffusion chain, weighted by the E-step tilt. Exact CVPO's per-iteration constraint satisfaction becomes satisfaction-up-to-\(\zeta\), and \(\zeta\) is invisible unless measured. Decision measurement: realized \(J_c(\pi_{\theta_{new}})\) vs the E-step target \(d_k\), tracked every iteration; if the gap is systematic, import QVPO's tight-bound weighting or increase T locally. This risk has no Gaussian analogue — it is the price of the policy class.

Severity: the guarantee-relevant risk. Mitigable, not removable.

Risk 2 — per-state duals amplify critic error

§5.2: \(\lambda^*(s)\) is a function of an \(M\)-sample self-normalized estimate whose effective sample size collapses under aggressive tilts, and it inherits \(Q_c\)-bias at full strength where the batch dual averages it away. CVPO's authors avoided per-state duals for exactly this reason. Decision measurement: shared-λ vs per-state-λ ablation at matched compute; keep λ(s) only if state-wise violation improves without inflating dual variance. Default remains shared λ — i.e. plain CVPO — until the data argues otherwise.

Severity: moderate; bounded by the discipline of defaulting to A (§5.3).

Risk 3 — the compute wall

CVPO is already the slowest safe-RL baseline on record (≈18× CPO's wall-clock in SDAC's Table 5); a K-step diffusion sampler multiplies the E-step's sampling bill by K. Without distillation the method may be correct and unusable. Decision measurement: wall-clock to a fixed (reward, cost) operating point vs SAC-Lag; the distilled-sampler variant (steps 4/10) must land within small-integer multiples of the Gaussian baseline or the massively-parallel-sim setting (TruDi's home turf) must absorb the difference.

Severity: practical show-stopper if ignored; engineering, not mathematics.

11.2 What v1 proposed that this chapter changed

Revision 1 — the multiplier is computed, not learned v2 · revised

v1 solves the dual by "a few steps of projected gradient descent" and describes \(\lambda_i\) as growing while violated and shrinking when satisfied — "the standard Lagrangian mechanism … inside the exponential weights." That phrasing quietly reintroduces the very object this whole approach exists to remove: multiplier dynamics, with a learning rate, lag, and windup. The chapter's §4 shows the dual \(g(\eta,\lambda)\) is jointly convex and is solved to the global optimum each iteration by warm-started SLSQP/Newton, at negligible cost beside the critic updates. Complementary slackness then holds exactly, per iteration — not asymptotically. v2 · new

Consequence: v1's PID-Lagrangian remedy is demoted to a fallback for the amortized \(\lambda_\phi\) variant only. For the scalar and bucketed cases there is nothing to oscillate — so nothing to damp.

Revision 2 — per-state λ(s) by regression, not dual ascent v2 · revised

v1's Variant B trains a multiplier network by \(\nabla_\phi\,\mathbb E_s\sum_i\lambda_{\phi,i}(s)\,(d_i-\mathbb E_q[Q_{c_i}])\) — gradient ascent on the per-state violation. This is exactly the mechanism ALaM (2026) documents as oscillatory: local overshoot propagates through the approximator to neighbouring states. §5.3 replaces it with the three-tier scheme — shared-\(\eta\) exact per-state solve (A), amortized \(\lambda_\phi\) fit by regression to solved KKT targets (B), or bucketed \(\lambda\) (C) — keeping v1's clever \(\lambda_i(s)=\bar\lambda_i\cdot\text{softplus}(f_{\phi,i}(s))\) correction form as the inductive bias for the regression. v2 · new

v1's own caution ("λ_φ noise may dominate") is upheld and quantified in §5.2: effective sample size collapses under aggressive tilts, and per-state λ inherits critic bias at full strength. CVPO's appendix pre-ran this analysis and declined the per-state dual for the same reasons — so Variant B is a hypothesis to test (§12), not a default.

Revision 3 — improvement budget as the default schedule v2 · revised

v1 lists a geometric anneal, a linear anneal, and the adaptive rule as alternatives. §6.2 promotes the adaptive rule to the default and formalizes it as the monotone cost-improvement budget \(d_{i,k}=\min(d_{i,k-1},\max(d_i^\infty,\hat J_{c_i}-\delta))\), with the feasibility and monotone-descent statements of Proposition 6.1. This also disposes of v1's open question "do the schedule and the \(\lambda\) dynamics fight?" — after Revision 1 there are no \(\lambda\) dynamics to fight with. v2 · new

11.3 What this chapter adds that v1 does not touch

Everything that makes the method a diffusion-policy method is new here v2 · new: the weighted denoising M-step and its weighted-ELBO bias (§7.2–7.3, Prop. 7.2), the path-space KL trust region reusing DIME/TruDi machinery (§8.1), the sampling-cost problem and its distillation fix (§7.4), and the inference-time HJ/CBF guard that converts soft expected-cost safety into a hard per-step guarantee (§8.2).
