# Off-policy mismatch in the TD(lambda) cost target (FH-DCMPO)

**Date:** 2026-08-19. **Status:** diagnosis complete; no correction implemented (by design).

**Question.** Cost-critic CVaR/VaR calibration is good when TD(lambda) is trained offline against
a frozen policy, but degrades badly online (s2b: VaR_0.9 coverage 0.77 vs 0.90 offline; predicted
CVaR 42 vs realized 82). Is the cause off-policy mismatch inside the L=64 TD(lambda) window --
behavior trajectories too different from the current policy?

## 1. What the code actually does (trace)

* **Storage** (`safe_rl/storage/replay_storage.py`): per transition, the buffer holds
  `observations, actions, rewards, dones, next_observations, costs, critic_observations,
  next_critic_observations, bootstrap`. **No behavior log-probs, no policy-version stamp** were
  stored before this investigation. Age IS recoverable from the ring pointer.
* **TD(lambda) window** (`_gather_cost_window`): for a sampled start `t`, returns the realized
  partial sums `sum_{k<j} c_k` over the next 64 stored transitions -- i.e. costs incurred under
  the **behavior policy's** actions `a_{t+1..t+j-1}`, frozen at episode boundaries.
* **Target** (`FHDCMPO._update_cost_critic_quantile`): `G_j = sum_{k<j} c_k + m_j Z_c(s_{t+j}, a')`
  with `a' ~ pi_current` (`sample_with_log_prob` at the bootstrap state). So the *bootstrap* is
  on-policy, the *window costs* are pure behavior-policy, and **no importance correction of any
  kind exists in this path** (`cost_is_weights` is hazard-stratification reweighting, unrelated
  to policy mismatch). Confirmed: the target is uncorrected for behavior/current mismatch across
  up to 63 intermediate behavior actions.
* **Scale of staleness**: a2 config collects 32 transitions/iteration (8 envs x 4 steps) into a
  1M ring, so the buffer spans **~31,250 iterations** of policy history; uniform replay means the
  median sampled window is ~15.6k iterations stale.

## 2. Mismatch measurement (checkpoint-pair emulation)

Behavior log-probs were not stored and **cannot be reconstructed exactly** -- the policy changes
every iteration, checkpoints exist every 5k, and optimizer/RNG state between checkpoints is gone.
Nothing was faked. Instead, `scripts/analysis/offpolicy_mismatch_probe.py` measures the mismatch
exactly on checkpoint pairs of the live a2 run (`20260819_080249`, reference pi_new = iter 45000):
roll out checkpoint `pi_old` (the behavior policy a replay item of age delta was generated by),
evaluate `log pi_new(a|s) - log pi_old(a|s)` on its trajectories -- both densities exact.

Age mapping: replay age delta_iters ~ {5k, 10k, ..., 40k}; the buffer's newest 10% is <=3.1k
stale, 10-25% is 3.1-7.8k, 25-50% is 7.8-15.6k, oldest 50% is >=15.6k.

20 episodes per checkpoint (20k states each, 2.3k windows each; sanity check: delta=0 reads
log-ratio exactly 0, ESS exactly 1, extreme fraction exactly 0).

**Per-step log-ratio vs age** (mean [p10, p90]; "extreme" = ratio outside [0.1, 10]):

| delta (iters) | replay bucket | mean lr | p10 / p90 | ratio<0.5 | extreme |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5,000  | newest ~16% | -0.87 | -2.85 / +0.60 | 43% | 14.5% |
| 10,000 | 25-32%      | -2.04 | -5.70 / +0.53 | 60% | 32.3% |
| 15,000 | ~50%        | -3.70 | -10.1 / +0.38 | 71% | 47.2% |
| 25,000 | oldest 20%  | -6.66 | -17.0 / +0.18 | 80% | 63.6% |
| 40,000 | (aged out)  | -10.4 | -25.5 / -0.40 | 88% | 75.6% |

**Cumulative window log-ratio and hypothetical-IS ESS, at delta = 5k (the NEWEST replay decile):**

| depth j | mean cum lr | p10 / p90 | ESS fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1  | -0.85  | -2.8 / +0.6   | 0.077 |
| 10 | -8.8   | -19.5 / -0.8  | 0.0023 |
| 25 | -22.0  | -44.2 / -5.0  | 0.0012 |
| 63 | -55.2  | -103 / -18.8  | 0.0014 |

So the mismatch is ENORMOUS in density-ratio terms: even the freshest replay data would retain
~0.2% effective sample size by depth 10 of an importance product. A full SDAC-style correction
over L=64 is arithmetically dead on arrival; only heavy clipping (which effectively shortens the
window) would keep any signal.

**But mismatch does not sit on the cost-bearing windows**: corr(|cum log-ratio|, window cost) is
-0.13..+0.05 across all ages, and corr with realized cost-to-go is -0.25..+0.13 -- essentially
zero. The drift is policy-wide (log-std shrinkage + mean movement), not hazard-specific.

**No age gradient.** The critic is roughly equally miscalibrated on its OWN policy's fresh data

"does calibration get worse specifically on old-policy trajectories?" is **no**. The apparent
qloss trend tracks the behavior policies' realized cost levels (38 -> 16 -> 30 across training),
not their age.

## 4. Control: full-mix vs recent-only critic retraining

Cost critic re-trained FROM the 45k checkpoint's weights -- frozen actor, identical TD(lambda)
rule (`_update_cost_critic_quantile`), identical update count and cadence (soft target update
every 4 cost grads) -- on (a) an age-mixed buffer emulating full replay (checkpoints 20k..45k)
vs (b) recent-only data (checkpoint 45k). Evaluated on held-out fresh pi_45k rollouts.

20,000 cost-critic gradient steps per arm, batch 256, identical everything except the data:

| arm | distinct transitions | level | VaR_0.9 coverage | CVaR pred/real | final loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| full_mix (ages 0-25k iters) | 119,976 | 1.35 | **0.899** | 56.5 / 47.0 | 184.5 |
| recent_only (age 0)         | 19,996  | 1.16 | 0.704 | 49.8 / 47.0 | 43.6 |

**The control refutes the hypothesis.** If off-policy multi-step mismatch were the cause,
recent-only should have improved tail calibration substantially. The opposite happened: the
maximally off-policy buffer landed at essentially nominal coverage (0.899 vs target 0.900) with a
mildly conservative tail, while recent-only under-covered (0.704) -- its far lower final loss
(43.6 vs 184.5) shows it memorized its 6x-smaller dataset instead. (The data-budget asymmetry is
deliberate: restricting online replay to the newest slice would shrink the dataset exactly the
same way.)

Two further facts the control establishes: (i) with a FROZEN bootstrap policy, mixed-age window
costs inject only a modest UPWARD bias (level 1.35, CVaR 56.5 vs 47.0) -- the opposite direction
from the online failure (s2b: predicted 42 vs realized 82, i.e. tail UNDER-read by 2x); (ii) the
same critic, same rule, same data distribution as online training reaches good calibration the
moment the policy stops moving -- which reproduces the "offline good" observation while using
maximally off-policy data.

## Verdict

**B -- the mismatch exists (and is enormous), but it does not explain the calibration failure.**

* Mismatch is real and monotone in age: extreme-ratio fraction 14.5% at delta=5k rising to 76% at
  delta=40k; hypothetical-IS ESS is ~0.1-0.2% by depth 10 for even the newest replay decile.
* But calibration error carries **no age gradient** (section 3): the critic misses the tail on
  fresh on-policy data (coverage 0.729, level 0.63) as badly as on stale data, and mid-age data
  reads best.
* And the causal control goes the wrong way for hypothesis A (section 4): recent-only data made
  tail calibration WORSE (0.704 vs 0.899), while a frozen policy trained on maximally off-policy
  data reached nominal coverage. Off-policy window costs bias the tail mildly UP; the online
  failure is a 2x UNDER-read.
* Mismatch is uncorrelated with cost magnitude (|corr| <= 0.25, mostly ~0), so it cannot be
  selectively corrupting the tail windows.

The offline-good / online-bad gap is therefore explained by **non-stationarity, not staleness**:
online, the bootstrap ``Z_c(s_{t+j}, a' ~ pi_current)`` and the E-step both chase a policy that
moves every iteration, and the critic lags it -- exactly the critic-lag hypothesis the a2/a3
(cost_critic_updates_per_step) arms are already testing. Answering the headline question: the
long TD(lambda) targets DO use behavior trajectories that are wildly different from the current
policy -- and, with gamma_c = 1 and the horizon feature making cost-to-go mostly state-dependent,
the critic turns out to be robust to that; it is not robust to the target moving.

**Consequences:**
* Do NOT implement SDAC-style importance-corrected TD(lambda): at ESS ~0.1% by depth 10, a
  64-step product is noise; clipping hard enough to fix that just reproduces a short-window
  target. The measurement kills experiment #3 of the original plan before it is run.
* Recent-replay-only TD(lambda) (experiment #2) is also refuted as a fix: it lost the control.
* The productive direction is faster critic tracking / slower policy movement (the running a2-a5
  arms), or target-side smoothing of non-stationarity (e.g. EMA/target-policy bootstrap), not
  off-policy corrections.
* The online `opd_*` telemetry (see below) stays worth one diagnostic run
  (`config/safety_gymnasium_fhdcmpo_a2_opd_diag.yaml`) to confirm the checkpoint-pair emulation
  matches the true mixed-age replay in vivo.

## Machinery added (all flag-gated, defaults preserve current behavior)

* `StochasticActor.log_prob(obs, actions)` + `action_log_prob` on the SAC/SafeSAC policy classes:
  exact log-density of a GIVEN squashed action (atanh inversion matching `sample`'s change of
  variables; boundary-clamped so replayed actions stay finite).
* `runner.store_behavior_logprob: true` -- stamps every stored transition with

* `FHDCMPO(offpolicy_diag=true, offpolicy_diag_interval=8)` -- logs `opd_*` telemetry every 8th

  on-policy bootstrap by construction), cumulative window log-ratio, hypothetical-IS ESS,
  extreme-ratio fractions, |log-ratio| by replay-age quartile, mismatch-vs-cost correlation.
* Config arm `config/safety_gymnasium_fhdcmpo_a2_opd_diag.yaml`: a2_lag_cc4 + both flags,

* Tests: `tests/test_offpolicy_diag.py` (log-prob inverse, flag-off inertness, extras shapes,

# Follow-up experiment 2: per-state cost spread matching (c1, 2026-08-20, queued after b2)

`scripts/analysis/qc_spread_probe.py` on the baseline's checkpoints (its own rollout states, the
E-step's exact proposal and readouts) measured the across-action structure of the cost signal:

| iter | std_a(Q_r) | std_a(rho_mean) | balance med (p90) | >lam_max | corr(Qr,rho) | TV@lam_max | inert frac |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5k  | 0.270 | 0.170 | 1.57 (12.0) | 46% | +0.51 | 0.126 | 27% |
| 20k | 0.183 | 0.150 | 1.26 (6.1)  | 36% | +0.64 | 0.123 | 28% |
| 45k | 0.086 | 0.100 | 1.03 (6.3)  | 31% | +0.61 | 0.109 | 34% |

Findings: (i) the cost spread is ~0.5-1% of the cost LEVEL; (ii) lambda_max cannot reach the
balance point at 23-46% of states and moves the weights by TV < 0.05 at ~30%; (iii) reward and
cost are positively aligned across actions (corr +0.5..0.7), so the penalty mostly shrinks
weights rather than re-ranking; (iv) the CVaR readout (kappa=1) carries 2-3x the spread of the
mean readout; (v) all spreads decay ~2.5x over training. A perfectly calibrated critic could not
steer the E-step at a third of states -- this, not the target plumbing, is the binding limit.

There is no cost normalization to remove (units are raw episodic by design), and a constant cost
scale is identical to raising lambda_max. The implemented response is per-state spread matching:
`CVPO(estep_cost_spread_match=true)` uses ``qc * s_b`` in the E-step exponent (and eta solve),
``s_b = std_a(Q_r)/std_a(Q_c)`` capped at `estep_spread_match_max` (10). lambda = 1 then buys
equal influence at every state. Levels (eqc, homotopy, feasibility, diagnostics) stay raw;
allowed only with the episodic grad-lambda controller, whose feedback never reads the exponent.
Arm: `config/safety_gymnasium_fhdcmpo_c1_spreadmatch.yaml` (a2 + the two keys). Logged:
`estep_match_scale_median/p90/scale_capped_frac` (note the key is `estep_match_scale_capped_frac`,
and it counts the UPPER cap only -- the lower clamp at `1/match_max` is not in it).

**c1 result (run 20260820_101946, stopped at 13k): idle-policy collapse.** Raw-ratio matching
multiplied TOTAL cost pressure by the batch balance (~1-2x and rising); with lambda's gains tuned

### Why c3 over-constrained: the readout dose, not a spread-match bug (2026-08-22)

The obvious suspect was a readout inconsistency -- `s_b = std_a(Q_r)/std_a(Q_c)` computed on the
MEAN readout while the exponent used the CVaR readout, which would have made the dose 2-3x too
large. **Audited: it is CONSISTENT, and the hypothesis is refuted.** The E-step takes the cost
readout exactly once (`cvpo.py` `_estep_weights`, `qc = self._estep_cost(...)`); `FHDCMPO`
overrides that one hook to return `rho_kappa`; `ratio = q.std(0)/qc.std(0)` and the exponent
`q - lam*qc_exp` and the eta solve all consume that same tensor. There is no second readout in
the E-step at all.

**Where the pressure actually lands.** `s_b` is median-normalized, so it is *exactly invariant*
to a global rescale of the readout: `ratio_s / median(ratio)` cancels the factor. Spread matching
therefore neither causes nor absorbs the CVaR switch -- the whole spread inflation multiplies
`lam * qc_exp`, against a `lambda_max: 1.8` episodic PID that is byte-identical between c2 and c3.
c3 raised the cost pressure several-fold and changed nothing that could compensate.

**Measured dose** = `median_s[std_a(CVaR_0.9 readout) / std_a(mean readout)]`, on c3's OWN
checkpoints (`qc_spread_probe.py`, new `dose_cvar_vs_mean_median` key; 1024 states x 64 actions;
all three seeds scored against seed 2's rollout states, `rollouts_20260820_203502_ep20.pt`):

| iter | seed 2 | seed 3 | seed 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5k | 2.97 | 2.32 | 6.37 |
| 10k | 10.00 | 10.00 | 10.00 |
| 15k | 3.77 | 8.41 | 7.75 |
| 20k | 2.91 | 7.65 | 5.20 |

So the "2-3x" the c3 arm was designed around was the OPTIMISTIC end: two of three seeds sit at
5-8x for most of training. At `lambda_max = 1.8`, an 8x dose is an effective multiplier of ~14 --
far outside the range the c2 gains were tuned for, and enough to explain the near-idle seed.

**The 10.00 at iter 10k is real, not a metric artifact.** For a NON-NEGATIVE cost,
`CVaR_alpha <= E[Z]/(1-alpha)`, so the dose is hard-capped at `1/(1-alpha) = 10`, with equality
iff all mass below `VaR_alpha` is exactly zero. At iter 10k the cost critic has collapsed toward
the softplus floor (mean level 1.93 vs 8.8-18.8 elsewhere) and **93% of the bottom-90% quantiles
are below 1e-3** -- the bound is saturated. Every seed hits exactly 10.00 there. A floored
`nonneg: true` quantile head converts a level collapse directly into maximum tail dose, which is
the worst possible moment for it.

**Now visible during training** (diagnostics only, no behavior change):
`estep_cost_dose_vs_mean_median` (the dose; exactly 1.0 on any `kappa = 0` arm, so c1/c2 stay
bit-reproducible), `estep_match_readout` / `estep_match_readout_is_tail` (the EFFECTIVE readout,
so a cvar arm still on the kappa warmup reports `mean`), and `estep_match_dose_vs_mean_median`
(`s_b_used / s_b_mean_based`). That last one reads ~0.98 on a live cvar run -- the standing
evidence that median-normalized spread matching does NOT absorb the dose, so the hypothesis above
does not need re-litigating. Note the runner filters `get_penalty_info` to numeric values, hence
the `_is_tail` companion to the string key.

**Fix implemented (c4, 2026-08-22): `estep_match_normalizer: reference`.** The normalizer, not
the readout, was the defect: `m(s) = cap(s_b/med_B[s_b])` divides by the ACTIVE readout's own
median, which is why it is scale-invariant and let the dose through. `reference` divides by
`med_B[s_b^mean]` instead, so the delivered grip `lam * m * std_a(C)/std_a(Q_r)` collapses to
`lam / M_ref` -- flat in the dose, equal to what the same lambda buys at kappa = 0. Default is
`active`, so c1/c2/c3 stay bit-reproducible; at kappa = 0 the two modes are bit-identical.
Headline diagnostic `estep_match_grip`; synthetic check (grip relative to the kappa=0 target):

| dose | active | reference |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.00x | 1.00x |
| 3 | 3.00x | 1.00x |
| 10 | 10.00x | 1.17x (50% of states on the clamp floor) |

**The cap truncates the correction at high dose.** Under `reference`, `m` has median ~1/dose
against a clamp floor of `1/estep_spread_match_max = 0.1`, so at dose ~10 -- exactly the
softplus-floor collapse above -- the median state sits ON the floor and the fix is silently
under-applied. New `estep_match_scale_floored_frac` makes it visible. Raising the cap is the
follow-up if it runs high through the ramp; c4 deliberately changes one thing only.

Arm: `config/safety_gymnasium_fhdcmpo_c4_cvar_refnorm.yaml` + `scripts/run_c4_arms.sh`
(3 seeds x 20k, matched to c3). Predictions and winner rule are pre-registered in the header.

**Consequence for the registered `fh_kappa: 0.4-0.6` knob.** `rho` is linear in kappa, so the
